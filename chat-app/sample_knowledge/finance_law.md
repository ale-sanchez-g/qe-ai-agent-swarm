## 🏠 Overview

If you and your partner separate, you need to consider what is to happen in relation to the division of any property that you own. The **Family Law Act** provides for property settlements between couples who are, or have been, married and also couples who have been in a de facto relationship. 

> **Important Note:** For the purposes of the Family Law Act, a de facto relationship includes a relationship between two people of the same sex.

### 🏛️ Legal Jurisdiction

The powers set out in the Family Law Act can be exercised by:
- **Family Court of Australia**
- **Federal Circuit Court of Australia** 
- **Local Court**

*The choice of court depends on the value of the property involved and the complexity of the matter.*

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### 🤝 Binding Agreements
**Q: If both parties have reached an agreement as to how the property will be divided, how can they be sure their agreement is binding and enforceable?**

In many cases, parties are able to reach agreement about a property settlement with the assistance of their lawyers. If they do, they can make an application for **consent orders**, which is a relatively straightforward procedure. 

**Benefits of consent orders:**
- ✅ Agreement is binding and enforceable
- ✅ Certain tax benefits are available

---

### ⚖️ When Parties Cannot Agree
**Q: What happens when the parties are unable to reach an agreement about the division of property?**

If you cannot reach agreement with your former partner regarding property settlement, you need to file an application for property settlement in the appropriate Court.

**Key facts about court proceedings:**
- 🏛️ Majority of property settlements are dealt with by either the **Federal Circuit Court** or the **Family Court**
- 🔄 There are ongoing opportunities for settlement after filing an application
- 📊 Most property settlement applications filed in court are settled **without** a court decision
- 👨‍⚖️ If settlement is not achieved, the Court will decide how property should be divided after a hearing
- 🏢 Complex matters are handled by the **Family Court of Australia** with specialized procedures and resources

## 📋 The 4-Step Property Settlement Process

**Q: What are the steps involved in an application for property settlement under the Family Law Act?**

When you apply for a property settlement, the Court determines the application by way of a “4-step” process.

Step 1 – Identifying and valuing the assets, liabilities and financial resources of the parties

This first step involves identifying and valuing **all** assets, liabilities and financial resources of the parties, whenever and however acquired.

> 💡 **Note:** In many cases this is a simple part of the process. However, particularly for matters involving businesses, the valuation exercise can be quite complex and require specialist experts.

---

### 🤝 Step 2: Assessment of Contributions
**Assessment of the contributions made by the parties**

The second step involves assessing contributions made by the parties during their relationship, including:

#### Types of Contributions:
- 💰 **Direct and indirect financial contributions** to the property of the parties
- 🏠 **Direct and indirect non-financial contributions** to the property of the parties  
- 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 **Contributions to the welfare of the family** including contributions in the capacity of homemaker or parent

#### ⚖️ When Contributions May Be Unequal

In many cases, particularly where there has been a **long relationship**, the Court will find that parties have contributed equally.

However, the Court may find **unequal contributions** in these instances:

| Scenario | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| 🕐 **Short relationship, no children** | Court focuses on direct financial contributions |
| 💎 **Significant pre-relationship assets** | One party entered with considerably more assets |
| 🎁 **Major individual contributions** | Inheritance, family gifts, or personal injury settlements |
| ⚠️ **Reckless conduct** | Deliberate or reckless conduct resulting in losses |

---

### 🔮 Step 3: Assessing Future Needs
**Assessing the future needs of each of the parties**

The third step involves assessing the future needs of each of the parties. The Court must consider factors including:

#### 📊 Key Considerations:
- 👥 **Age and state of health** of each party
- 💼 **Income, property and financial resources** and capacity for employment
- 👶 **Child care responsibilities** for children under 18 years
- 💸 **Support commitments** to self or others
- 🏦 **Pension and superannuation eligibility**
- 🏠 **Reasonable standard of living** in the circumstances
- 📈 **Impact on earning capacity** from the relationship
- 👫 **New relationships** and their financial circumstances

> ⚖️ The Court will decide whether an adjustment is needed to compensate for differences in future circumstances.

---

### ✅ Step 4: Fairness Assessment
**Is the proposed division of property fair to both parties?**

After assessing steps 1-3, the Court must then decide whether the proposed division of assets is fair to each of the parties. 

> 🏛️ **Holistic Assessment:** This evaluation examines all circumstances of each case comprehensively.

---

## 📝 Pre-nuptial and Pre-relationship Agreements

**Q: Is it possible to have a binding pre-nuptial or pre-relationship agreement?**

The **Family Law Act** provides for **binding financial agreements** to be made between parties to a marriage or de facto relationship. 

### ⏰ Timing Options:
- **Before** the marriage or de facto relationship
- **During** the marriage or de facto relationship  
- **After** the end of the marriage or de facto relationship

> 💡 **Particularly Attractive For:** Parties entering into a second or subsequent marriage/relationship with substantial assets.
